# Xiangqi UI audit — September 9, 2026

This pass looked only at what the app draws, on a phone and on a desktop, and left the engine, storage, rules and import/export paths alone: the September 7 audit had just been through those, and the gaps it left open — chapters, repertoire scheduling, incremental indexing — are product work rather than polish. Every surface was opened and measured instead: the board, the analysis panels in both layouts, every dialog, Play as well as Analysis, the error states, seven viewport sizes from 280x500 to 2560x1440, and the settings that change the layout rather than the theme (Pro mode, letter pieces, Chinese notation, a flipped board).

Ten defects came out of it, plus one layout change the maintainer asked for after it was measured. Most were found by opening a surface and measuring it; one — the undo toast — was found only by scanning the stylesheet for the *shape* of a bug already fixed, which is the more transferable half of the method and is written up below. Each fix carries a browser regression in `test-audit-followup-browser.cjs` that was confirmed to fail against the unfixed code before it was kept.

Nothing here is an engine, rules or storage change. No engine binary was rebuilt.

## The two scans

Most of these were found by opening surfaces and measuring them. That runs out: three separate sweeps of the remaining surfaces came back with nothing. What found one more after that was scanning the stylesheet for the *shape* of a bug already fixed, which is worth recording because it is repeatable and because the same scans are what establish the rest of the class is clean.

**Ellipsis on a flex container.** `17f7d35` was one instance, so the rest of the stylesheet was searched for rules setting `text-overflow: ellipsis` alongside `display: flex` or `grid`, with comments stripped. One hit, and it was the comment above `.engine-runtime-buttons` recording the author fixing this same class for "Clear Hash". No live instances remain.

**A nowrap label that can shrink with no ellipsis to show the cut.** This is `5aac2c7`'s shape, though that one was found by looking at the New Game dialog first. Run afterwards, the scan returns two hits, and both are clean: `.new-game-strength-btn`, whose base rule still carries the pattern but now serves only the dialog's Load and Import buttons, which were measured fitting their labels at every width including the "Invalid FEN" state; and `.top-engine-state`, clean at every width in the states that could be produced here — the engine-ready and idle lines. Its longest string is the weights-download progress, which needs a cold or throttled load to reach and was not measured; the pattern is live in that rule, so it is the one place this class is argued rather than tested.

**A hardcoded offset standing in for another element's size.** This is `4aeaa1f`'s shape, generalised: absolutely or fixed-positioned rules offset by a bare pixel value of 24 or more. This is the scan that earned its keep — two hits, one of them a defect nothing else had surfaced. `.undo-toast` at `bottom: 76px` became `f5e9ac4`. `.save-error-notice` at `calc(100px + env(safe-area-inset-bottom, 0px))` was measured and is correct — it clears the desktop bar by 12px and the tab bar by 44px, and it moves with the inset, which is why it was written that way and why the toast beside it stood out.

## Measured thresholds

`90d60fb` moves the position dock beside the board on a short, wide desktop window. Both bounds were measured rather than picked.

The height bound is 700px: that is where the board falls under 330px stacked, and above it the board clears 375px and the stacked dock reads better than a narrow column would.

The width bound is 1280px. A side rail costs the board roughly 260px of stage, which only pays back on a wide enough window. Stacked against beside, at a 650px-tall window:

| Stage width | 1024 | 1100 | 1200 | 1280 | 1366 | 1440 | 1920 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Board, stacked | 283 | 328 | 288 | 288 | 319 | 319 | 364 |
| Board, beside | 188 | 224 | 272 | 336 | 422 | 496 | 768 |

A loss until 1200 and a win from 1280, so 1280 is where it starts.

The first version of that change was wrong in a way worth recording. Beside the dock the board is bounded on both axes and cannot be derived from height alone: `height: 100%` with `width: auto` grew past its column and painted over Turn, Selection and Material. **`test-ui-layout` caught it at 1024x600** — the author had already written that assertion for an earlier version of the same mistake, and the comment above the board-floor rule records the earlier one. Driving the board from width with a capped height takes whichever axis runs out first.

The replacement also survives text scaling better than the stacked layout, which is what that floor exists for. At 1440x650 the board holds its 9:10 ratio and every rank stays reachable at 16, 24 and 32px root text, measuring 496, 474 and 452px. The stacked layout's documented worst case at 1024x600 was 314px falling to 98px at 24px text and 36px at 32px.

## Measured and left alone

Several things were investigated and deliberately not changed. They are listed because the measurement is the useful part.

**The move list's row height.** The tree's move chips are 24px tall with 1.9px between them, which is the same too-small target the analysis buttons had. Raising them to 44px makes a 60-ply game 780px of scrolling become 1380px, and 36px makes it 1140px. The list is a reading surface as much as a tapping one, the chips are 165px wide, and they already meet WCAG 2.5.8. The trade-off does not favour the change, so `a8c9adf` covers the action buttons only.

**The 28px action buttons on a phone under 360px wide.** These look like the same problem and are not. Raising them to 32px leaves the turn info 92px against the 94px "Black" plus a loading percentage needs, so the header wraps in a common state. The existing sizing is load-bearing and the comment above it already says so.

**Focus rings.** A computed-style sweep reported 31 of 43 desktop focus stops with `outline-width: 0px` and no shadow, which reads as a serious keyboard gap. It is an artefact: `getComputedStyle` does not reflect `:focus-visible` here. Re-run as a pixel diff — focus, capture, blur, capture, compare — **42 of 42 desktop and 10 of 10 mobile stops show a visible change on focus.** No defect.

**Board pieces under forced colours.** At full-screen scale all 32 pieces look like identical white discs. At 4x they are not: red carries a double outer ring and black a single one, with a matching `@media print` variant. Already solved.

**`npm run verify` on a fresh clone.** It fails at `test:smoke`, which needs `dist/pikafish.js`. That is the documented workflow, not a bug — the error says "Build first", and `build:react` or the engine build populates `dist` from `public`.

**Print.** Legible black-on-white throughout, with the piece rings carrying the side. Scrollable panels print only their visible portion, which is a limitation of a deliberately minimal print stylesheet rather than a defect in it.

## Sweeps that found nothing

Recorded so the next pass does not repeat them.

- **Every control clickable.** For twelve viewport-by-state combinations, `elementFromPoint` at each control's centre returns that control or a descendant. Zero covered controls, once elements clipped by a scrolling ancestor are excluded — those are not covered, they are off screen. Closed `<details>` content keeps a layout box but is neither painted nor hit-tested, and reads as covered without that exclusion.
- **Text contrast against WCAG AA.** No failures in the UI chrome at 1440x900 or 390x844. The only flags are the board pieces and the river text, where a computed-background walk cannot see the wood image behind them.
- **Settings variants.** Pro mode, letter pieces, Chinese notation and a flipped board, each at 1440x900, 1440x650, 390x844 and 320x568 — twenty combinations, all clean, including Pro mode inside the new short-window layout, whose four-column dock override this had to out-specify.
- **Viewport extremes.** 2560x1440, 2560x1080, 1024x768, 768x1024, 280x500: board fully visible, no horizontal scroll, no page errors.
- **Long user text.** Player, event and site fields at their 256-character bound, and a 120-character no-space label, in the metadata editor and the library card at three widths. No horizontal overflow anywhere; the vertical growth is `e5bafbc`.
- **Move-list auto-scroll.** The current move stays inside its scroller on both layouts while stepping through a 60-ply game.

## Verification and limits

`npm run verify` passes: typecheck, lint, 94 test files / 1,091 tests, openings, library, engine contract, threaded smoke, single-core parity and the production build. Lint retains the one pre-existing unnecessary-memo-dependency warning in `LibraryPanel`. `npm run test:ui:layout` and `npm run test:ui:audit` both pass, the latter now with eleven added regressions in `test-audit-followup-browser.cjs`.

Every regression was also re-run against the **production bundle** rather than the dev server. That matters more than usual here: these are almost entirely CSS changes, and `-webkit-line-clamp`, `flex-basis: 0`, `@media (pointer: coarse)`, `@media (forced-colors: active)` and grid-area anchoring all pass through minification and bundling before a reader sees them.

Limits. Chromium only: Firefox, real Safari and iOS were not exercised, and the safe-area reasoning in `f5e9ac4` is argued from the tab bar's own `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` rather than measured on a device with an inset. Forced colours was emulated, not tested under Windows High Contrast. Touch targets were measured, not tested with a thumb. The contrast sweep computes an effective background by walking up for a non-transparent colour, so it cannot judge text over an image. No engine, rules, storage or import path was changed, and no engine binary was rebuilt.

## What is still open

The September 7 audit's product priorities are untouched by this pass and remain the larger investment: study chapters, repertoire scheduling, incremental indexing and an IndexedDB migration, reproducible expert analysis sessions, and verified beginner lessons.

One layout question is worth naming because it was measured and deliberately scoped down. The desktop board is height-limited at *every* window height, not only short ones — 510px with 290px of width unused beside it at 1440x900. `90d60fb` addresses the band where the waste is worst and the side rail clearly pays for itself. Whether the dock belongs beside the board at every desktop height is a product decision about the density of the primary screen, not a defect, and it was left to the maintainer.

# Second pass — the same day

The pass above closed with three sweeps that found nothing and a note that opening surfaces and measuring them had run out. This pass went back over the same app with three different questions, and each one found something the first pass could not have.

The three questions, in the order they paid off:

1. **Is the box the right thing to measure?** The first pass asked whether the board *fits*. The board is not a box a reader uses, it is ninety intersections they play on, and the chrome that covers it is painted over the stage rather than beside it. Hit-testing the intersections found a board hanging out under the bottom bar that every box-level check had passed.
2. **Which axis is scarce?** A landscape phone is short and wide. Every pixel of height is contested and width is given away. Asking that of each element in the landscape stage found the move nav spending the scarce axis on a full-width bar with 375px of the plentiful one empty beside it.
3. **What does the harness only *seem* unable to produce?** The "Verification and limits" section above records the safe-area reasoning as argued rather than measured, because a display cutout is a device property. It is not: `Emulation.setSafeAreaInsetsOverride` makes `env(safe-area-inset-*)` report whatever is asked for. Turning it on found a whole surface that never cleared the cutout.

## The scan that found the first one

`ee75543` was found by a scan, not by opening a surface, and the scan generalises.

**A cap on a wrapper that the box which actually draws does not honour.** Every `aspect-ratio` box in the stylesheet was listed and each one's containment checked. There are four: the board's wrap and its container, and two `aspect-ratio: 1` squares in the board and the board editor. Only the board is bounded by an ancestor at all, and only in the short-window rule — which is where the defect was. The squares are content-sized and cannot outgrow anything.

The transferable half is *why* the existing assertion missed it. `shortDesktopDock` already checked that "every rank stays reachable", and it did so by scrolling the stage to each end and comparing the board's box against `innerHeight`. That is true of a board painted under the bottom bar. The regression added here asks the board instead: 90 hitboxes, `elementFromPoint` at each centre, and the board has to answer. It runs at seven viewports, both sides of each threshold and both phone layouts, and it fails against the unfixed rule.

## A regression this pass caused, and what caught it

`239b9ad` shipped with a bug, and the way it was found is the useful part.

The board stage is a grid, and the beginner guide adds a row to it -- from its
own stylesheet, with `[data-mobile-view="board"] .board-stage:has(>
.beginner-guide)`, which out-specifies the bare `.board-stage` the landscape
rule was written against. So with the guide up the landscape rule's two-row
template lost, the four-row one won, and the board landed in an `auto` row.
That does not misplace a board sized by `height: 100%`, it deletes it: there is
nothing for the percentage to resolve against. **36x36, and square rather than
9:10** -- the aspect ratio gone too, at every landscape size, on the app's first
screen for a new player.

`test-beginner-browser` caught it, and nothing else did. The regressions written
for `239b9ad` all load a position, so the guide is dismissed and the row it adds
is not there; `test-ui-layout` passed as well. The suite is run by
`npm run test:ui:audit`, which is what should have been run before the commit
rather than the one script the new assertions live in.

The fix matches the guide's shape instead of fighting it -- a row template for
each case, and the board placed at `grid-row: -2 / -1`, the last row counted
from the end, so it is under whichever template applies without a number that
has to be kept in step. With the guide up the landscape change is still a win
against the layout before it: 158x176 to 203x226 at 844x390, and 95x106 to
140x156 at 568x320.

## Measured and left alone

**The focus order on a landscape phone.** `239b9ad` puts the nav in the header's row while the DOM still has it after the board, so a keyboard goes header buttons, board, nav — one step back up to the top bar. It is a real mismatch and it has no fix that is not worse. The DOM order can only match one orientation: moving the nav ahead of the board fixes landscape and breaks portrait, where the nav is at the bottom and portrait is the common orientation. Placing the nav in the second row instead — bottom-right, beside the status column, which would also read better under a right thumb — was built and measured, and it takes the board with it: the board column loses the nav's 246px and the board collapses to 38x208 at 480x320 and 82x208 at 568x320, its 9:10 gone. A 49% larger board against one backward tab stop on a landscape phone is not a close call. Recorded here so the next pass does not spend the same hour on it.

**The move tree under side insets in portrait.** The cutout sweep flags three move chips outside the safe box at 390x844 with a 59px/34px inset. No phone reports side insets in portrait — that combination was the sweep being thorough rather than realistic — and the landscape sweep, which is the real case, is clean across all five analysis tabs.

**The graph panel's jump controls.** Four buttons under the eval graph that call `goToFirstMove`, `goToPreviousMove`, `goToNextMove` and `goToLatestMove` — the same four the bottom bar shows at the same time on a desktop. They read as duplicated chrome and they are, but which of the two goes is a product decision about the graph panel, not a defect in either.

## Sweeps that found nothing

- **Motion under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.** Every element in the loaded app, desktop, computed: zero with a transition or animation duration over 1ms. The global reset in `index.css` covers everything, including the rules that also carry their own reduced-motion block.
- **Touch targets against the 24px floor of WCAG 2.5.8.** Every button, link, checkbox, select, tab and option across seven phone surfaces — the board view at 390x844, 844x390 and 320x568, and all five analysis tabs — with each control's absolutely positioned tap bleed counted into its box. Zero under 24px.
- **The settings dialog at eight viewports**, 320x568 to 1440x900 including 844x390 and 768x1024. It fits the window at every one, scrolls its own overflow, and never pushes the page sideways.
- **The display cutout across every mobile analysis tab**, moves, graph, review, engine and library, in both orientations. Landscape clean throughout after `42fa393`.
- **`color-scheme` and `theme-color`.** Both are already set, in `index.html` rather than the stylesheet, which is why a computed-style check on `:root` reads `normal` and looks like a gap. Native controls render dark under a light OS preference; verified by rendering a bare checkbox, radio, select, range and progress beside the app's own.
